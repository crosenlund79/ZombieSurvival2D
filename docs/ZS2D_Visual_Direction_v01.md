# ZombieSurvival2D — Visual Direction v0.1

**Status:** Approved reference for overall palette and atmosphere; other visual decisions remain exploratory.  
**Source:** [Moodboard v0.1](ZS2D_Moodboard_v01.png)  
**Note:** Concept/mood art, **not** an example of the in-game camera or a requirement to use side-view, isometric, or cinematic framing.

## Visual north star

*A beautiful world reclaiming itself after humanity's collapse. Human shelters feel handmade, practical, and worth returning to; distant AI-controlled threats feel precise, alien, and dangerous.*

## Carry forward from the reference

- **Palette:** Muted natural greens, olive and moss, faded gold and warm straw, earth brown, aged rust, soot/charcoal; occasional cool machine lighting. Preserve gentle color variation; avoid turning the whole surface brown or gray.
- **Surface mood:** Mostly quiet melancholy; overgrown woodlands, farmland, marshes and rural outskirts. Nature is alive even where humans have disappeared.
- **Danger gradient:** Near scorched fringe and AI patrol boundaries, vegetation thins, colors become ashen, silhouettes sharpen, and atmosphere shifts toward low-grade dread. Annihilated zones are lethal, not playable urban maps.
- **Bunker mood:** Warm amber practical lighting, improvised repairs, worn steel/concrete/wood, personal objects and accumulated comfort. Bunkers are plausible survivable spaces, not sleek sci-fi sets.
- **AI mood:** Crisp utilitarian silhouettes, colder materials and light, intimidating precision; deliberately out of place among organic overgrowth.
- **Illustration:** Hand-drawn non-pixel art, expressive irregular ink, textured surfaces and restrained illustrated shadows; readable at gameplay scale and practical to reproduce with a drawing tablet.

## Do NOT infer from the reference

- Do not copy the board's cinematic viewpoint, framing or character proportions into gameplay.
- Do not lock top-down vs shallow-angle 2.5D, world/bunker spatial relationship, sprite dimensions, tile size, lighting pipeline, or rendering implementation.
- Do not make every biome scorched or desaturated.
- Do not treat all depicted POIs, biome examples, equipment or props as approved gameplay content.
- Do not infer zombie design from this board: deliberately deferred.
- Do not substitute the tone of Gothic horror, retro pixel art, or generic Fallout wasteland.

## World context (for visual consistency)

A rogue AI escaped confinement and launched catastrophic strikes on population centers. Settlements were erased into craters; survivors live mostly beyond active AI patrols. Surface regions comprise distinct biomes. Scattered bunkers/vaults, many ruined or contaminated by later drone attacks, preserve evidence of a desperate effort to survive. A few active shelters may refuse outsiders. AI drone attacks near the player are rare but possible. This context informs visual design; do not expand it into new canon without design discussion.

## Collaboration and decision handling

- Treat the moodboard as **an approved direction for palette and feeling**, not an approved camera or content specification.
- Preserve existing game-design APPROVED items; log unconfirmed details as EXPLORING.
- When making future mockups, keep the palette and material language consistent while translating them into whichever gameplay perspective is eventually selected.
- Prioritize visual readability, asset reuse, and a hand-drawable workload.
